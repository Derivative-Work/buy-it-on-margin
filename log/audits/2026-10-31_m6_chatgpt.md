# Fact-check verdict — 31 October 2026

**Overall assessment: HOLD FOR CORRECTIONS.**

The historical framing works as satire, and several of the financial figures have credible support. However, there are important problems with the disclosure card, the distinction between Thursday's Alphabet collapse and Friday's wider technology selloff, and the presentation of historical margin rules.

The biggest editorial risk is that viewers could mistake a satirical comparison for a claim that the present market is experiencing another 1929-style crash. The video needs to preserve the distinction between a severe technology-stock correction and a systemic financial crisis.

I have treated 31 October 2026 as the fact-check cutoff. I distinguish between:

- **ACCURATE:** Supported by the available evidence.
- **MISLEADING:** Contains a factual basis but creates an incorrect impression.
- **WRONG:** Contradicted by evidence or materially misstated.
- **UNVERIFIABLE:** Insufficient reliable evidence to establish the claim.

### Important distinction about N25

The headline is defensible, but I recommend making the market-value distinction explicit in the end credits or accompanying description.

The October 30 reporting supports a technology-stock market-capitalisation wipeout exceeding $1 trillion. The S&P 500 technology sector fell more than 4.2% during the session.  Yahoo Finance

Suggested alternative:

> TECH STOCKS: MORE THAN $1 TRILLION IN MARKET VALUE ERASED IN ONE DAY

This is more precise without sacrificing the newsreel effect.

### N31: the historical comparison

The two dates are correct.

The Federal Reserve's historical account establishes:

- September 3, 1929: the Dow reached its peak.
- October 28, 1929: Black Monday.
- October 29, 1929: Black Tuesday.
- July 1932: the Dow reached its low, approximately 89% below its peak.
- November 23, 1954: the Dow finally regained its September 1929 closing high.  Federal Reserve History

One subtlety: the crash did not single-handedly cause the Great Depression. It was an important part of a much larger economic collapse.

I would retain the four-second silence. It makes the distinction between the dates theatrically effective without implying that the two events were economically equivalent.

## 2\. The Anthropic disclosure: N34a

This is the most important card to get right because it concerns the company whose AI model helped write the lyrics.

### What the SEC filing establishes

Anthropic's October 26, 2026 Form S-1 is genuine. I located the filing in the SEC's EDGAR database.  SEC+1

The financial statements disclose:

| Figure | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| First-half 2026 net loss | **$80.748 billion — accurate** |
| Fair-value changes in convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs | **$72.388 billion — accurate** |
| Relationship to company valuation | The filing says these changes were primarily driven by increases in enterprise valuation. |
| Adjusted net income | **$2.671 billion profit**, despite the reported GAAP net loss. |
| Future infrastructure commitments | The $518 billion figure requires careful qualification; it should be presented as contractual commitments, not expenditure already incurred. |

The crucial accounting distinction is that the $72.4 billion is not simply an ordinary operating expense or cash loss. It is principally a fair-value adjustment to financing instruments. The filing explicitly connects the adjustment to increases in Anthropic's enterprise valuation.  SEC+1

There is another important number worth knowing: Anthropic reports adjusted net income of $2.671 billion for the first half of 2026, after excluding specified accounting and other adjustments. That does not make the GAAP loss disappear, but it is important context.

**My recommendation:** Don't describe Anthropic as having simply burned $80.7 billion in cash. That would be materially misleading.

### IPO status

As of October 31, 2026:

- Anthropic has filed an S-1.
- The filing proposes a Nasdaq listing.
- The proposed ticker is ANT.
- The registration statement is not yet effective.
- The preliminary prospectus does not yet establish a final offering price or completed public listing.

The SEC filing itself says the securities cannot be sold until the registration statement becomes effective.  SEC

Therefore, the line:

> Why, its maker just filed to sell stock.

is **ACCURATE**.

It does not claim that Anthropic is already publicly traded.

However, the following distinction matters for release: an S-1 filing is not the same as an IPO having taken place. Recheck the SEC filing status immediately before publication.

### Recommended replacement for N34a

I would make the disclosure more technically precise:

- Identify the $80.7 billion as a reported GAAP net loss.
- Explain that $72.4 billion arose from fair-value changes in financing instruments, primarily associated with increased enterprise valuation.
- Identify the $518 billion as committed future infrastructure spending, rather than cash already spent.
- State that the IPO registration is pending effectiveness.

This is a disclosure, not a reason to remove the satire. In fact, the accounting distinction makes the satire more interesting.

## 4\. Specific problems with N34b and N34c

### N34b: Where 1929 doesn't fit

**Verdict: ACCURATE**

The historical comparison is well constructed.

The Dow's approximately 89% decline and November 1954 recovery are supported by the Federal Reserve.

One small improvement: say "the Dow regained its 1929 peak in 1954" rather than simply "it took until 1954 to recover."

That makes clear what recovered: the index's nominal pre-crash closing level, not the entire economy or investors' inflation-adjusted wealth.

### N34c: Margin requirements

**Verdict: MISLEADING WITHOUT A QUALIFICATION**

The 1920s comparison is reasonable.

The current 50% initial margin requirement is established by Regulation T for most ordinary margin purchases of margin securities.

However:

- It is an initial margin requirement, not a universal rule for every investment.
- Brokerages can impose stricter requirements.
- Maintenance margin requirements are different.
- Other financing arrangements can operate under different rules.

The card also makes an important economic distinction: today's AI infrastructure spending is not simply millions of small investors borrowing to buy shares.

Large profitable companies are financing much of the buildout.

That is a good point, but don't imply that all AI investment is funded from profits or that large corporations cannot suffer serious financial distress.

**Suggested replacement:**

"1920s buyers often put down about 10%; today's Regulation T rules generally require 50% initial margin for ordinary margin-stock purchases. Much of today's AI infrastructure spending is financed by large companies with substantial revenues and cash flows, rather than small investors buying shares on credit."

## 5\. Investment-advice and editorial risks

There is one issue I would address before release.

The chorus repeatedly says:

> Buy it on margin, sugar, buy it on margin\!

This is clearly intended as a period-style satirical sales pitch. Nevertheless, taken out of context, it is an imperative encouraging leveraged investing.

My assessment:

- Full video: **LOW RISK**, given the historical framing and explicit disclaimer.
- Shorts version: **LOW TO MODERATE RISK**, because viewers may see only the chorus and its associated visuals.
- Promotional clips containing only the chorus: **MODERATE RISK**.

I would retain the SH00 disclaimer.

For additional protection, consider adding a small persistent caption during the chorus:

"SATIRE — HISTORICAL REFERENCE — NOT INVESTMENT ADVICE"

The end-card disclaimer is also appropriate.

Importantly, the disclaimer does not excuse factual inaccuracies elsewhere.

## 7\. Final release checklist

My recommendations, in order of importance:

1. **Keep the Anthropic disclosure**, but explain the accounting adjustment properly.
2. **Retain the $1T headline**, preferably adding "market value" for precision.
3. **Keep both date cards.** Both dates and weekdays are correct.
4. **Qualify the Great Depression comparison** so the crash is not presented as its sole cause.
5. **Clarify the 50% margin rule** as an ordinary Regulation T initial-margin requirement.
6. **Label OpenAI's $856B and $278B figures as projections.**
7. **Retain the shoeshine legend qualification.**
8. **Recheck Anthropic's IPO status immediately before release.** An S-1 filing can become effective, be amended, or be withdrawn.
9. **Preserve the investment disclaimer**, particularly in the Shorts version.

### My overall verdict

**The video is factually defensible as satire, subject to the accounting and margin-rule qualifications above.**

The strongest parts are the 1929 historical comparison, the actual Alphabet earnings figures, the October 30 technology-stock selloff, and the distinction between speculative retail margin buying and today's institutional AI infrastructure financing.

The weakest part is the potential impression that Anthropic literally consumed $80.7B in cash. That is not what its reported net loss means.

The most important distinction to preserve throughout the film is this:

**A technology-stock selloff, an AI infrastructure financing problem, and a systemic financial crisis are three different things.**

The film is strongest when it makes audiences wonder whether history rhymes, without telling them that history has already repeated itself.
