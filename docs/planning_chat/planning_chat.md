## 18. Claude (2026-10-31T09:23:15.875899Z)

The newsreel look is a gift here, because nearly everything that makes it read as 1920s is cheap to fake: black and white, low frame rate, grain, flicker, intertitles, and no synced sound. The fastest approach is mostly stills and code, with generated video only where real motion sells the joke.

**1. Code does the "film": all shots, minutes to render**

Do every period effect procedurally in Remotion (or ffmpeg filters) over whatever imagery you have:

- 18 fps, with frames held to get that hand-cranked judder
- Gate weave (a small random jitter), brightness flicker, grain, dust and scratches, vignette
- Iris wipes, and a film-burn transition for the cut
- Every intertitle card, in an Art Deco font and frame
- Over-cranking for the final chorus, which just means playing frames faster

This layer turns clean images into a newsreel, and it's deterministic. Fixed seeds keep it reproducible like the rest of the series.

**2. Stills with camera moves: most of the shots, seconds each**

Generate black-and-white stills locally on the 5090: the barbershop, the cornfield building, the nested offices, the banker's dial, the empty hall for the coda. Then animate them with slow pushes and pans. Newsreels leaned on exactly this kind of slow camera move, and grain and weave hide how static the image is. Generate several takes per shot and pick fast; at seconds per image you can afford to.

**3. Short generated clips: only the 4–6 shots that need motion**

Use LTX-2.3 rather than Wan, since it's much faster. Render short, low-resolution clips (2–4 seconds at 480p), because the newsreel softness forgives upscaling. Spend them where movement is the joke:

- Dancers doing the Charleston, which can be looped and re-cut across both choruses
- Ticker tape streaming down
- The dancers' freeze before the burn, which can just be a held frame of a dance clip

**4. Cut-out silhouettes: an alternative to generated dancers**

If the dance clips come out mushy, animate black paper-cutout figures in code instead: jointed silhouettes doing a looped Charleston kick against a white backdrop. That's a real 1920s animation technique, renders instantly, and always stays on-model. It also suits the series' code-rendered videos.

**What the format saves you**

- **No lip sync.** Silent newsreels didn't have synced sound, so the singers never need to move their mouths in time. That cuts out the hardest and slowest part of the other videos.
- **No sharp detail.** Faces, hands and text can be soft or absent, and the grain covers generation artifacts.
- **No text generation.** Intertitles carry the jokes and facts, and they're added in the edit.

**One shortcut to consider carefully**

Public-domain 1920s newsreel footage, such as the Prelinger Archives, would be the fastest and most authentic option. But it breaks the series' generated premise, it shows real people, and real 1929 crash footage risks the "no real Depression suffering" rule. If you use it at all, keep it to object inserts like a real ticker machine or trading-floor paper, check each clip's rights, and log it as a human intervention.

**A fast order of work**

1. Lock the audio and build the timing map.
2. Build the Remotion film layer and intertitle cards first, so every later asset drops straight in.
3. Generate the stills in one batch, then the handful of LTX clips overnight or in the background.
4. Cut, apply the film layer, render.
